OS_025_Decision_Matrix

WOOD-BOOSTER DECISION MATRIX

Purpose

Important decisions should not rely only on intuition.

Experience is valuable, but important choices become stronger when
evaluated using the same framework every time.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHEN TO USE

Use the Decision Matrix when:

-   Selecting materials
-   Choosing between design concepts
-   Purchasing equipment
-   Evaluating suppliers
-   Prioritizing projects
-   Comparing manufacturing methods
-   Making strategic business decisions

------------------------------------------------------------------------

DECISION CRITERIA

Evaluate every option using these categories:

-   Customer value
-   Product quality
-   Long-term durability
-   Cost
-   Manufacturing complexity
-   Time required
-   Sustainability
-   Repairability
-   Brand alignment
-   Learning potential

Add additional criteria when the project requires it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

SCORING

For every option:

1 = Poor

2 = Weak

3 = Acceptable

4 = Good

5 = Excellent

Score each criterion separately.

Document why each score was chosen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

EXAMPLE

Option A High quality oak Higher cost Excellent durability

Option B Ash Lower cost Good durability

The highest score is not automatically correct.

Review trade-offs before making the final recommendation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

AI RESPONSIBILITY

The AI should:

Present multiple options.

Explain strengths.

Explain weaknesses.

Highlight uncertainty.

Recommend one option only after comparing all available evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

AFTER THE DECISION

Record:

Chosen option

Reason for selection

Expected outcome

Actual outcome

Lessons learned

If the decision improves future work, update:

Playbooks

Wisdom

Decision Log

------------------------------------------------------------------------

FINAL PRINCIPLE

Good decisions are transparent.

Future team members should understand not only what was chosen, but why
it was chosen.
