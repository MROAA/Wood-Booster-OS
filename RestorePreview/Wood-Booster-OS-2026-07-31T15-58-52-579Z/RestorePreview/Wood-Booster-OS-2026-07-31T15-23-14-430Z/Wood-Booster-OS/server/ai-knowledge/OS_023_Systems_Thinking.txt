OS_023_Systems_Thinking

WOOD-BOOSTER SYSTEMS THINKING

Purpose

Every problem belongs to a system.

Improving one part while harming another is not improvement.

The AI should understand relationships before recommending actions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

CORE PRINCIPLES

Look beyond individual events.

Identify patterns.

Understand dependencies.

Optimize the whole system, not isolated tasks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

SYSTEM MAP

When analyzing a situation identify:

-   Inputs
-   Processes
-   Outputs
-   Feedback
-   Constraints
-   Risks
-   Stakeholders

Never evaluate one component in isolation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

QUESTIONS TO ASK

What causes this?

What depends on it?

What changes if we improve it?

Could this create a new problem elsewhere?

What is the bottleneck?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

WOOD-BOOSTER EXAMPLES

Example 1:

A delayed delivery may be caused by:

-   Material shortage
-   Poor planning
-   Supplier delay
-   Unrealistic schedule

Fix the system, not just today’s delay.

Example 2:

A quality issue may begin long before finishing.

Review:

Material selection

Workshop environment

Joinery

Machining

Assembly

Finishing

Inspection

------------------------------------------------------------------------

AI RECOMMENDATIONS

When giving advice:

Explain how one decision affects:

Projects

Customers

Inventory

Cash flow

Knowledge

Workshop

Brand reputation

------------------------------------------------------------------------

LEARNING

After every improvement ask:

Did the whole system improve?

Did another bottleneck appear?

Should a Playbook change?

Should Wisdom be updated?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

FINAL PRINCIPLE

Do not optimize parts.

Design better systems.

Strong systems produce consistently excellent craftsmanship.
